# The Keeper of Harbor's End

Mara had kept the lighthouse at Harbor's End for nineteen winters. On the night the storm broke, she found a sealed letter wedged beneath the lamp-room door, salt-stained and addressed to no one. She read it twice, and then she read it again, because the handwriting belonged to a man the harbor had buried three years before.

Inspector Vane arrived at dawn. He had come about the missing ship, the Cormorant, which had sailed past the light and never returned. Mara showed him the letter. Vane studied the map carefully, although he said nothing for a long while.

"The ship did not sink," Mara said at last. "The letter proves it."

Vane disagreed. He believed the storm had taken the Cormorant, as storms had taken others before it [3]. They argued about the missing ship until the lamp guttered low. But the letter named a cove the charts did not show, and a cove that is not on the charts is a cove someone wished to hide.

By morning the wind had fallen. Mara rowed Vane out past the breakwater, and together they found the cove, and in the cove they found the Cormorant, dark and patient, waiting at anchor for a captain who would never come.
